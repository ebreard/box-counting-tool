What D is

The image is turned into two classes by a threshold on one channel inside the mask. Boxes of side s are laid over it at 16 grid origins (4 per axis) and N(s), the mean number of boxes that hold the outline (Interface) or any foreground pixel (Set), is counted for box sides about 6 per octave. D is the slope of log N against log 1/s over the widest range of sizes on which that line is straight: drift (slope of the upper half of the range minus the lower half) at most 0.08, rms residual at most 0.03 in log10 N, at least 6 sizes over at least 0.7 decades, at least 30 boxes per size and at least 8 boxes across the mask. A range that fails these checks is flagged, and a flagged D is the least bad window, not a dimension. This is the box-counting (Minkowski-Bouligand) dimension over a finite range of scales; it equals the Hausdorff dimension only for special sets.

Interface counts the pixel edges between unlike pixels, so a mask and its complement give the same count and the mask border makes no outline. Set counts foreground pixels: use it for thin curves and dust, not for a filled region (a region with area has dimension 2 at small boxes).

D_err is a precision (spread over windows and grid origins), not an accuracy. On the benchmark images D was off by up to 0.1, usually low: quote D to plus or minus 0.1 at best.

Luminance, object region and noise check

Luminance (Rec. 709) is 0.2125 R + 0.7154 G + 0.0721 B, the weights of rgb2gray in scikit-image, rounded to 8 bits. Grey is the Rec. 601 luminance, 0.299 R + 0.587 G + 0.114 B; the two differ by a few grey levels where the colour is strong.

Object region (Region: drawn mask, object only) narrows the drawn mask to the object inside it, on the chosen channel before the pre-blur: the pixels darker (or brighter) than the Otsu threshold of the mask, their largest 8-connected piece, a closing by a 9 x 9 square, holes filled, then an erosion by a disc of radius k px (k = 0: no erosion). Pixels outside the drawn mask stay out. The region then replaces the mask everywhere: fit, threshold scan, suggested settings, robustness and calibration. Its edge is a mask border, so it makes no outline: only the contours inside the object are counted, at least k px from its edge.

Noise check (kappa): noise of 1 grey level (standard deviation, on 0 to 255) is added to the channel after the pre-blur, twice with independent draws, and each copy is thresholded like the photo. kappa is the share of the first contour that lies within 1 px of the second, corrected for chance: about 1 when the noise barely moves the contour, about 0 when it moves it at random. Below 0.5 the smallest boxes measure noise at this threshold rather than the outline. It is shown with the fit, for each level of the threshold scan and in the robustness text; it does not change the robustness verdict.

Suggested settings (rules fixed in advance)

  • Channel: the one whose two classes separate best inside the mask: Otsu's eta, the between-class variance over the total variance at the Otsu threshold, largest of the eight channels.
  • Threshold: Otsu inside the mask on that channel.
  • Pre-blur: 0, or 1 px when more than 10 % of the outline belongs to pieces of 1 or 2 pixels (speckle from noise or compression).
  • Mode: Interface, unless one class is made of thin lines or dust: mean thickness 2 A / P of 2.5 px or less, A its area and P the length of the outline. Then Set, with that class as the foreground.
  • Smallest box: 2 x blur + 1 px (the blur kernel); at least 4 px on Saturation, Blue minus Red and Blue / Red of a JPEG, whose colour is stored at half resolution in 8 x 8 blocks; otherwise automatic.
  • Work resolution: full, or 1/2 above 12 megapixels.
  • Warnings: no mask; the largest piece above the threshold holds less than half of that class (a texture, not one outline) or less than 90 % of it (the other pieces add outlines of their own); eta below 0.7 (the threshold is a choice); a colour channel of a JPEG. For one outline, draw the mask as a band along the edge, with both sides of it inside.

None of these rules uses the value of D, so they cannot steer D towards 4/3 or 5/3.

Robustness

D is counted again with one choice moved at a time: the threshold moved so that the foreground area changes by 5 and 10 points of the mask either way, the pre-blur one pixel up and down, the work resolution halved, the smallest box doubled and quadrupled, and each half of the mask (split at the median column and the median row of the mask pixels). The other mode is shown for information and left out of the verdict, because it measures a different object. The local slope inside the fit window of the current result is read too: a dimension needs a plateau.

  • Robust: every change moves D by 0.05 or less, no run is flagged and the local slope stays within 0.15 inside the fit window.
  • Sensitive: a change moves D by more than 0.1 (the benchmark accuracy), or more than two runs are flagged, or the local slope spans more than 0.25 inside the fit window, or the current fit is flagged.
  • Moderately sensitive: in between.

Calibration with synthetic outlines

Gaussian random fields with spectral density proportional to k^-(2H + 2) are fractional Brownian surfaces whose level lines have dimension 2 - H: H = 2/3 gives 4/3, H = 1/2 gives 3/2, H = 1/3 gives 5/3. Four fields of each (seeds 1 to 4) are made over the bounding box of the mask, scaled to 8 bits, box-blurred like the photo, thresholded so that the same share of the mask is foreground, counted in the same mode inside the same mask and fitted over the same window as the current result. The measured D of these known outlines shows how far this chain reads low or high, and whether it can tell 4/3 from 5/3 at all. The fields have no compression, no perspective and no shading: they test the counting chain, not the physics of the photo.

From a photo to a 3D surface

A surface of dimension D3 cut by a plane gives an outline of dimension D3 - 1 when the surface is isotropic, so 7/3 and 8/3 become 4/3 and 5/3. A photograph of an opaque cloud shows a projection (a silhouette and the shading of the near side), not a cut, so the relation is an assumption.
